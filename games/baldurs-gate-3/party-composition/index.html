Baldur's Gate 3 Party Composition

Think in party roles first, then decide which classes or companions fill them.

BG3 gives you many ways to build a four-character party, and several classes can cover more than one job. That makes role coverage more useful than copying one fixed “best party.”

Five useful roles to think about

  • Frontline: survives pressure and keeps dangerous enemies occupied.
  • Single-target damage: removes priority targets quickly.
  • Area damage or control: handles groups and limits enemy movement.
  • Healing or defensive support: keeps the party functioning through difficult encounters.
  • Utility and skills: covers locks, dialogue, exploration and non-combat checks.

One character can cover several jobs

A Cleric can support and control, a Rogue can provide utility and damage, and a Wizard can combine area damage with control. Do not force one party slot to equal one role.

Start from the gap, not the class

If the party keeps dying in melee, add frontline durability or control. If fights drag on against one dangerous enemy, improve single-target damage. If exploration feels awkward, add skill coverage rather than swapping characters only for combat numbers.

Use companions and respec flexibly

Companion story preference and party role do not have to conflict. BG3 allows class changes, so you can keep the companion you want and adjust the combat job when needed.

Recommended workflow

  1. Assign each current party member a primary job.
  2. Run the Party Role Planner.
  3. Identify the one gap that is actually causing problems.
  4. Open Classes, Companions or a specific build page to solve that gap.
